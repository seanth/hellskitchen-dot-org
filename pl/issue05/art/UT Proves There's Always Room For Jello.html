UT Proves There's Always Room For Jello

Written By Saul Rosenrosen

In order to keep a large audience captive for a long stretch of time, one needs the ability to craft words in a manner that is both critical and satirical. The founder of the Alternative Tentacles record label, Jello Biafra, showed that when we are mentally stimulated, maybe we do not have the short attention spans that commercial television would like us to have. Keeping a capacity crowd of about a thousand for over four hours, Biafra proved that we crave an intellectual, fresh point of view. Seen as a threat by politicians, corporations, and the music industry, Jello Biafra showed the standing room only crowd at the Texas Union Ballroom just how much bullshit we need to look through day to day.

Biafra, former front man of the Dead Kennedys, led the audience into an inescapable perspective of the corruption that plagues this country daily. One wonders if Jello was born with this talent or if he became jaded from Tipper Gore's Puritan crusade against his band, his record label, and the First Amendment -oops! I mean her crusade against that which is obscene. (It was Gore who created the "Parental Advisory" label seen on so many of our favorite albums today.)

Jello spoke about everything from freedom to his prophecy (maybe wishful thinking?) that the huge corporate machine will collapse and the people will rise above it, if we're prepared:

We're not equal 'till we're equal. We're not free until we're free. I'd rather work for something I want and not get it than work or vote for something I don't want, and get it. What we've got now, I repeat, there's no way this can last. Wake up and smell the noise. Corporate dictatorship is headed for a train wreck, and that train wreck will happen in our lifetime.

Sometimes serious, often humorous, Jello also talked about companies like Nike and Levi Strauss closing their plants in the United States because they "aren't making enough money." Then the executives give themselves forty million dollar bonuses, open plants in Thailand or China, and pay their new labor a mere eight to forty cents per hour.

Jello also talked of his own childhood in the show, saying that his parents raised him without hiding anything from him. His parents did not ignore him when he asked questions about the news. Nor did they belittle him by saying that he should not watch the news. Jello's parents told him Greek mythology for bedtime stories, and they actually raised him instead of hiring someone to raise Jello for them.

Thanks to the amazing cooperation given to Piggy Lovin' by Alternative Tentacles, we were able to ask Jello a question or two. This rather impresses us; that a man can speak almost non stop for four and a half hours, and still talk to his fans after the show. Now that we have given credit where credit is due, we thank Jello. His new spoken word album, If Evolution Is Outlawed, is available in stores for $16.99, but don't let the price fool you: that's three CDs of pure Jello.
