The Best Movies You've Never Seen

Written By Schwa, the unknowable letter

As we here at Piggy Lovin' march oh-so-bravely into another semester of University life, I thought it would do well by all concerned for us to take a quick look at a film about college-sort of.

This issue's Best Movie You've Never Seen is:

- Oleanna -



For those of you who don't know much about David Mamet, it's okay-he's not the best-known writer/director around. Of course, you may very well have exposure to him and just not know about it. Do the names The Edge, The Spanish Prisoner, or Ronin ring a bell? Well, you know more about this guy than you might think. He wrote all of them, and even directed The Spanish Prisoner. Suffice it to say that he's been doing this for a while, and he's damn good at his job. I could talk about any of these, but I've decided to spend some time with one of my personal Mamet favorites-Oleanna.

"But wait," you ask, "what's the movie like?" To which I'd smugly reply that it's a lot like the play of the same name. But for those of you who would still look at me cross-eyed, I might be able to put it to you this way-I had a director once who said 'Mamet shows us human beings' infinite capacity for cruelty.' Oleanna is a very simple story. Not a whole lot happens, really. There aren't dozens of interesting locales-there's no hip soundtrack by contemporary pop stars, or even a stunning score by a gifted composer. Like most Mamet productions, it takes place for the most part on a single set. Nothing explodes. It has a cast of two. And they're not supermodels. And they don't have sex.

Which is how I come to the point. They don't have sex. Remember that, because it's important. Go ahead, write it down if you need to-there's no harm in taking notes. William H. Macy (Fargo), a talented but, as of yet, untenured professor at a prestigious university is accused of sexual harassment. I won't tell you why, I won't tell you how-that would ruin how Mamet works.

Which is how I come to the warnings section. The dialogue is high-brow. It's traditional Mamet-short, clipped lines. Be forewarned. It's not a movie for everyone-after all, if it was, you wouldn't be reading about it here, now would you? This movie could be very hard to understand if you're not paying attention. The wars of the classes and the sexes aren't topics everyone can sit through. If you can, though, do it. It's tense, it's intense, and it's just plain good.
