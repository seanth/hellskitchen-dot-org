Part Three of the Saga of the Official Bureau of Weights and Measures


As we last left our hero (hero referring to me in this situation), I had just coasted into Santa Clara, California, home of the Hewlett Packard plant where they produced the 32 cesium atomic clocks employed at the official center of time in Washington DC. It was my mission to take over this center for time, and this plant was the place to start. My assets included ninety-four dollars and change, a half a tank of gas, a car that really belonged to my parents, and my Swiss army knife. I was not discouraged, however. This would hopefully be sufficient for supplies to take over the world's time.

I began by getting a P.O. box at the post office so I could have a make-shift local address, and then I signed up for a membership at the nearest gym (which I made sure also had a gym in Washington, for when the time came for me to go there). It was then time for me to get a job. I pulled into the parking lot of the Hewlett Packard plant, found a parking space right near the front, and walked right in. I was going to work for these guys for a few months, maybe a year, and snoop around until I knew enough about these clocks that I was going to control one day, and then I would go to Washington with the money I had made and take over the Official Center of Time. While I worked at the plant, I would live in my car, pick up my paychecks at the post office, work out and practice my martial arts every day (mind you I was fairly well acquainted with a couple different styles of fighting, but proficient with none), and take all my showers at the gym.

Once I walked inside the plant, I came to a security desk by a set of elevators where a portly man with a badge sat and smugly smiled at me.

"What can I help you with, son?"

"I am here about an employment position."

"You mean you want to work here?" I suppose he was confused or something.

"Yessir. If you could just tell me what room to visit, I'll be on my way."

"How old are you, boy?"

"Eighteen." The man gave me a funny sort of "I don't want to deal with you" look and pushed a button on his desk.

"Mr. Johnson, there is a young man here seeking employment. What should I tell him?"

"Not Hiring."

"OK." He smiled a smug little "sorry" smile and then spoke. "Sorry."

"Thank you."

I had not expected to be blown off. I didn't know why, but I had never thought I couldn't get hired there. I figured I could at least get a job sweeping floors and eventually come across a room that said "secret" or "DO NOT ENTER," or something, and break in and find out the last clue I needed to take over the Center for Time. Life never quite works like the movies.

Instead, I drove down the road a bit to a little café where you could drink coffee and play on the internet at the same time. They hired me to work there full time, and things started to look a little bit better. I decided to spend a lot of time at the library or at work on the internet studying anything I could about time, atomic clocks, or the official Center for Time. Apart from the part where I worked at the plant, my plan went well. I would just work and study and work out until I had enough money to go to Washington. The whole super-spy thing where I snuck in and found the clues was pretty silly anyway. I wouldn't even know what to look for. So I worked for the money I would need to take over the world.

And that is where this piece of the saga ends. A sad note, where my plans have fallen short, and at this point sleeping in my car seems much less worthwhile than when I was going to be working for the plant, but I couldn't complain, and I couldn't go home. I also couldn't figure out what I was going to do when I did get to Washington.
